# Excess Refund — Section 73 Demand

## What it means
If you have received a **GST refund in excess** of what you were entitled to, or received a refund to which you were not entitled at all, the department can initiate demand proceedings under **Section 73 of the CGST Act, 2017** to recover the excess.

## Common triggers
- Refund claimed on exports but goods not actually exported
- ITC refund claimed on inputs not used for exports
- Refund of taxes not actually paid (zero-rated supply refund on non-zero-rated supplies)
- Arithmetic errors in refund application leading to excess refund
- Refund claimed under wrong category (e.g., refund of duty paid on inputs vs. refund of integrated tax)
- Duplicate refund claims

## Recovery process
1. Department identifies excess refund through scrutiny or audit
2. Show Cause Notice (DRC-01) issued under Section 73
3. Taxpayer has 30 days to reply
4. If demand confirmed, order passed under Section 73(9)
5. Excess refund recovered through:
   - Adjusting against current tax liabilities
   - Deducting from future refund claims
   - Recovery proceedings (Section 79)
   - Interest at 18% per annum from date of refund

## Response required
- Verify if refund was indeed excess
- Provide evidence that goods were exported (shipping bills, LUT)
- Show that ITC was used for export purposes
- If error accepted, pay the excess with interest
- Contest with evidence if refund was correct

## Statutory reference
- **Section 73(1)** — Demand for excess refund
- **Section 73(9)** — Time limit: 3 years from date of refund
- **Section 54** — Refund of tax
- **Section 55** — Refund in certain cases
- **Rule 89** — Application for refund
- **Section 50(1)** — Interest on excess refund

## Severity
High — Excess refunds must be returned with interest. Department can recover from your current/future tax liabilities or bank accounts. 3-year limit applies.
