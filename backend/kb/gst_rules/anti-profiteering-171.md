# Anti-Profiteering — Section 171

## What it means
**Section 171 of the CGST Act, 2017** mandates that any reduction in tax rate or benefit of ITC must be passed on to the consumer through commensurate reduction in prices. Failure to do so is "profiteering."

## Common triggers
- Tax rate reduced but MRP not reduced proportionally
- ITC benefit not passed on to consumers after GST implementation
- Price increase higher than the tax rate increase during transition
- New ITC eligibility (e.g., under Section 16) not reflected in retail prices
- Anti-profiteering investigation triggered by consumer complaint or suo motu

## How anti-profiteering works
- National Anti-Profiteering Authority (NAPA) / Competition Commission of India (CCI) investigates
- compares pre-GST and post-GST prices, adjusting for cost changes
- If the price reduction is less than the tax reduction, it's profiteering
- Profiteered amount is computed and ordered to be refunded to consumers
- Or deposited in Consumer Welfare Fund

## Response required
- Maintain detailed cost records and pricing data
- Demonstrate that benefits were passed on (price reduction = tax reduction)
- Show that any price increase is due to genuine cost increase, not profiteering
- File response within the time specified in the notice
- Provide purchase invoices, MRP declarations, price lists, and cost breakdowns

## Statutory reference
- **Section 171** — Anti-profiteering measure
- **Rule 126-137** — Anti-profiteering rules (National Anti-Profiteering Authority)
- **Section 171(3A)** — Penalty for profiteering (up to ₹25,000 + 10% of profiteered amount)
- **Section 171(3B)** — Penalty for failure to furnish information

## Severity
Very High — Can result in large refunds to consumers, penalty up to ₹25,000 plus 10% of profiteered amount, and public naming. Reputation damage is significant.
