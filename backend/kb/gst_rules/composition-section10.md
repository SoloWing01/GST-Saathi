# Composition Taxpayer — Section 10 Violation

## What it means
The **Composition Scheme** under **Section 10 of the CGST Act, 2017** allows small taxpayers to pay a fixed percentage of turnover as GST instead of regular filing. Violating the conditions of this scheme can lead to penalties and demand notices.

## Conditions of Composition Scheme
- Turnover limit: ₹1.5 crore (₹75 lakh for special category states)
- Cannot make interstate supplies
- Cannot supply goods through e-commerce operators
- Cannot collect tax from recipients (supply must be without tax collection)
- Cannot claim Input Tax Credit
- Cannot supply non-GST goods or services (except exempt)
- Must file quarterly CMP-08 and annual return GSTR-4

## Common violations leading to notices
- Exceeding turnover limit but continuing under composition scheme
- Making interstate supplies while on composition
- Collecting GST from customers (illegal for composition dealers)
- Failing to file CMP-08 quarterly statement
- Claiming ITC while on composition scheme
- Supplying through e-commerce operators

## Consequences of violation
- Cancellation of composition registration
- Demand for regular tax (not composition tax) for the period of violation
- Interest on tax not paid at regular rates
- Penalty under Section 122 for contravention
- Liability to file regular returns (GSTR-1, GSTR-3B) for the period

## Statutory reference
- **Section 10** — Composition levy
- **Section 10(2)** — Conditions for composition scheme
- **Section 10(4)** — Turnover limit
- **Section 10(6)** — Restriction on collecting tax
- **Section 122** — Penalty for contravention
- **Rule 5** — Registration under composition scheme

## Severity
Medium-High — The main risk is being moved to regular scheme and having to pay the difference between composition tax and regular tax for the violation period, plus interest and penalty.
