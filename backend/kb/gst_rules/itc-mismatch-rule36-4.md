# ITC Mismatch — Rule 36(4) Violation

## What it means
When your Input Tax Credit (ITC) claimed in GSTR-3B exceeds the eligible credit available in GSTR-2B as auto-populated from your suppliers' GSTR-1 filings, it constitutes a **Rule 36(4) violation**. The excess ITC is denied.

## Common triggers
- Suppliers not filing GSTR-1 → ITC not reflecting in GSTR-2B
- Suppliers filing incorrect amounts in GSTR-1
- Claiming ITC on invoices not uploaded by suppliers
- Timing differences between your return filing and supplier's filing
- ITC claimed on exempt or non-GST supplies
- ITC reversal not done for lost/stolen goods or free samples

## Rule 36(4) limits
- ITC claim in GSTR-3B cannot exceed 105% of ITC available in GSTR-2B (for invoices where supplier hasn't filed GSTR-1)
- This limit was initially 110%, then reduced to 105%
- Only applies to invoices where supplier hasn't uploaded details
- Does not apply if ITC is available in GSTR-2B

## Typical response required
- Reconcile ITC claimed vs. ITC available in GSTR-2B
- Follow up with suppliers to file GSTR-1 with correct invoice details
- Reverse the excess ITC in subsequent returns if genuinely over-claimed
- Provide proof of invoices if ITC should be available (supplier issued invoice but didn't file)

## Statutory reference
- **Section 16(2)(aa)** — ITC available only if details furnished in GSTR-1
- **Rule 36(4)** — Restriction on ITC claims
- **Section 16(2)(d)** — ITC subject to conditions prescribed
- **Section 50(3)** — Interest on excess ITC claim
- **Section 73/74** — Demand proceedings for excess ITC

## Severity
Medium-High — Excess ITC must be reversed with interest. If not reversed voluntarily, demand proceedings may follow.
